# Saputo Law editorial direction

## Voice

The existing voice is defiant, direct, and skeptical of government power. Keep that conviction, but remove repetition and unsupported superlatives.

Use:

- Short, declarative sentences.
- Specific evidence: a court, result, publication, credential, or year.
- Calm confidence instead of repeatedly saying "best," "relentless," or "award-winning."
- Plain descriptions of what happens next.
- One clear action at the end of each section.

Avoid:

- "Warrior" language on every page.
- Long biographies inside unrelated pages.
- Awards without a year or source.
- Generic SEO introductions.
- More than one primary call to action per section.

## Insights & Recognition

Use one landing page with three distinct sections:

### Legal Insights

Firm-authored, practical explanations of Texas criminal law. Feature no more than six recent or evergreen pieces. Link the full archive separately.

Intro:

> Clear answers about Texas criminal law, written by the attorneys who use it in court.

### In the Press

Third-party interviews, quotations, and profiles. Display the publication name, subject, and date when verified. Do not imply an endorsement.

Intro:

> Journalists turn to Saputo Toufexis for context on criminal prosecutions, constitutional rights, and the limits of government power.

### Honors

Show only recognitions with a source and year. Separate peer-reviewed honors from client-review awards.

Intro:

> Recognition from legal peers, independent publications, and clients.

## Results

Keep results on a separate page. Each entry should contain:

- Year
- Court or county
- Charge or issue
- Outcome
- A short explanation of the defense work

Place this disclaimer above and below the list:

> Prior results do not guarantee a similar outcome. Every case is different.

- Tell us what happened
- Meet the attorneys
- Explore our practice
- Read the legal guide

Avoid vague labels such as "Learn more," "Click here," and "Submit."
